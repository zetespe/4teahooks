# Market research: crochet pattern-tracking apps (2025-2026)

Research date: 2026-10-01. Sources are app store listings, vendor sites and help centres, review blogs, and support forums. Reddit could not be reached directly (the research tooling blocks it), so complaints come from app store reviews, vendor forums, Trustpilot, and blog reviews that summarise community sentiment. Treat prices as approximate and region-dependent.

## 1. Landscape at a glance

The market splits into five groups:

1. **Pattern marketplaces with interactive readers**: Ribblr, and Ravelry with its third-party apps. Patterns are interactive only if the designer authored them on the platform.
2. **PDF-markup trackers**: knitCompanion, My Row Counter, YarnBuddy, Stash2Go, Pattern Keeper (cross-stitch). The pattern stays a PDF, and the app adds highlighters, counters and notes on top.
3. **Simple counters**: BeeCount, Easy Knitty, StitchTally, YarnTally, Crochet Pal and dozens of near-identical "Row Counter" apps. They count numbers and know nothing about the pattern.
4. **AI pattern parsers and explainers (new in 2025-2026)**: Hooked, Crochet Pal for Beginner, Crocheteer, YarnUP, Yarnie, CrochetPop, Knot a Problem. They turn pasted text, a PDF or a photo into step-by-step rows, offer "plain-English" explanations, and are almost all subscription-based.
5. **Learning and kit ecosystems and chart tools**: The Woobles (video micro-steps) and StitchFiddle (chart design).

## 2. Product notes

### Ribblr (iOS, Android, web)
- **Pricing:** The app is free with ads and a daily limit on free patterns. Ribblr+ costs about $4.99, $6.99 or $9.99 a month across three tiers. Ribblr+ removes ads and the limit, adds monthly "Gems" for paid patterns, and lets you add external patterns. [ribblr.com/subscribe_menu](https://ribblr.com/subscribe_menu), [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/ribblr-crochet-knitting/id1529605339)
- **How patterns get in:** Designers author "ePatterns" in Ribblr's builder (Ribbuild), which can also convert PDFs. [Ribblr: best crochet app](https://ribblr.com/learn/best-crochet-app)
- **Tracking:** You tap a line to mark it done, and tapping "Rep"/"Repeat" opens a stitch counter. "Smart Sizing" shows only your size, and some lines have embedded video tutorials.
- **Accounts and privacy:** An account is required, and purchased patterns are viewable only in the Ribblr app or website. The designer decides whether a PDF can be downloaded. [Ribblr forum](https://meet.ribblr.com/t/how-to-save-pattern-as-pdf/152420), [forum](https://meet.ribblr.com/t/downloading-a-pattern-from-here/275362)
- **Complaints:** Buyers can't print or download what they paid for and can't use patterns where Wi-Fi is blocked (lock-in). Users also complain about ads and paywalls. [Trustpilot](https://ca.trustpilot.com/review/ribblr.com)
- **Takeaway:** This is the closest UX to ours (line-by-line ticking, size filtering, repeat counter), but it only works for Ribblr-authored patterns and needs an account and connectivity.

### Ravelry and its third-party apps (web; apps by other developers)
- Ravelry is a free, account-based pattern database and library with no official mobile app or built-in tracker. Third-party apps connect to it, including Unraveled (iOS), My Row Counter, Stash2Go II (rebuilt in November 2025, with PDF markup and watch counting) and YarnBuddy. [ravelry.com/about/apps](https://www.ravelry.com/about/apps)
- **Complaints:** The 2020 redesign caused reports of migraines, vertigo and seizures. Ravelry then restored "Classic Ravelry" as the default, which is a lasting lesson in accessibility (avoid high-contrast flicker and give users control). [Daily Dot](https://www.dailydot.com/irl/ravelry-disabled-website/), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ravelry)
- **Takeaway:** Ravelry is where patterns are *found*. Following them still means a PDF or a web page.

### knitCompanion (iOS, Android)
- **Pricing:** The Basics tier is free. Setup+Essentials costs about $0.99 a month or $9.99 a year, or roughly $30 as a perpetual licence. [knitcompanion.com](https://www.knitcompanion.com/), [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/knitcompanion-knitting-more/id1058142783)
- **How patterns get in:** It works with any PDF (from Ravelry, Dropbox and similar).
- **Tracking:** Row highlighter bars, linked counters, "Magic Markers" for counting and colouring stitches, scribbling on the pattern, and a timer. It works offline.
- **Complaints:** A steep learning curve. Some users watch tutorials for over an hour before starting, and the interface feels cluttered. [handsoccupied.com](https://www.handsoccupied.com/knitcompanion-intro/), [grand-screen review](https://grand-screen.com/apps/knitcompanion/)
- **Takeaway:** This is the power-user standard. It has the most features, but the setup cost is high and it is knitting-chart oriented.

### My Row Counter: Knit & Crochet (iOS, Android)
- Imports PDFs and web pages, with direct Ravelry access, multiple counters, chart highlighting and PDF annotations. Rated about 4.8 on Google Play with 15k+ reviews. [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.row_counter&hl=en_US)
- **Pricing:** Freemium with ads. Premium costs about $2.99 a month or $9.99-14.99 a year.
- **Complaints:** Intrusive banner and pop-up ads (some reportedly NSFW), and the Apple Watch counter sits behind a subscription. [App Store reviews](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/my-row-counter-knit-crochet/id1342608792?see-all=reviews&platform=iphone)

### YarnBuddy (iOS, iPadOS, macOS, watchOS)
- A project tracker with custom fields (yarn, hook), counters, and PDF annotation and highlighting. Row notes pop up at specific rows.
- **Pricing:** The free tier allows 10 projects. Pro costs $14.99 a year or $44.99 one-time. [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/yarnbuddy-knit-and-crochet/id1267678125), [Peer Reviewed](https://www.peerreviewed.io/blog/yarnbuddy-the-project-tracking-app-for-knitters-and-crocheters)
- **Takeaway:** "Row notes that appear at row N" is a well-liked idea worth copying.

### BeeCount (Android, free and open source)
- Multiple projects, each with several counts. Counts can be **linked**, so "rows" can drive pattern-repeat counts at different rates. It also has row alerts, auto-reset at a value, notes, and **full database backup and restore**. [F-Droid](https://f-droid.org/en/packages/com.knirirr.beecount/), [knirirr.com](https://knirirr.com/beecount/)
- **Takeaway:** The privacy-respecting, free, backup-file model already has fans, but BeeCount has no pattern text at all.

### Generic row and stitch counters (Easy Knitty, StitchTally, YarnTally, Crochet & Knit Stitch Counter, Crochet Pal and others)
- These offer big tap targets, multiple counters (row plus stitch, which matters for amigurumi decreases), sometimes PDF storage, voice or "hands-free" counting (YarnTally), and timers. Advanced counters are often behind premium. [Easy Knitty](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/easy-knitty-row-counter-knit/id1557552992), [StitchTally](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/stitchtally-row-counter/id6738016114), [Crochet & knit stitch counter](https://apps.apple.com/bw/app/crochet-knit-stitch-counter/id1545599414)
- **Crochet Pal** (Android, a university project) explicitly positions itself against "lots of ads, overly complicated interfaces, and lack of accessibility". [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=cpp.edu.crochetpal&hl=en_US)
- **Complaints: data loss.** An Easy Knitty user lost all projects and the queue. Another user paid for PDF storage in "Crochet row counter & patterns", and the PDFs vanished after they had deleted the originals. [Easy Knitty, Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=fr.whatsup_design.easyknitcounter&hl=en_US), [lv.crochet.app](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=lv.crochet.app&hl=en_US)

### AI pattern parsers and explainers (2025-2026 wave)
- **Hooked - Crochet & Knitting** (iOS, Android): imports PDFs with "smart parsing", accepts pasted text or a Ravelry library, and turns them into a step-by-step "Work Mode" with counters attached. "Smart Explain" gives plain-English explanations of terms. Hooked Pro (about $39.99 a year, 3-day trial) is required for unlimited explanations, imports and projects. [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/hooked-crochet-knitting/id6757484781)
- **Crochet Pal for Beginner** (iOS and Watch): imports from web links or photos and turns patterns into step-by-step checklists. [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/crochet-pal-for-beginner/id6771014727)
- **Crocheteer**: breaks patterns into parts and rounds that you tick off. [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/crocheteer-crochet-tracker/id6755787507)
- **YarnUP**: a "Stitch Explainer". You paste a row, tap a token such as `[sc, inc] x 6`, and get instructions, a chart symbol and a video. [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/yarnup-crochet-for-beginners/id6782724537)
- **Yarnie**: scans printed patterns and explains every abbreviation. [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.ai.crochet.knitting.stitch&hl=en_US)
- **Knot a Problem and CrochetPop**: market "never lose your place" row trackers. CrochetPop stresses showing only your size in multi-size patterns. [knotaproblem.app](https://knotaproblem.app/), [CrochetPop blog](https://learn.crochetpop.app/blog/live-row-tracker-track-any-crochet-pattern)
- **AI pattern *generators*** (Yarn AI, Crochet AI and others) are widely mocked. Patterns generated by ChatGPT produce "monstrosities" because the model can't do stitch arithmetic. AI-made images are also used to sell bad patterns on Etsy. [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2023/07/08/us/chatgpt-crochet-patterns-artifical-intelligence-cec/index.html), [AOL/Etsy](https://www.aol.com/news/etsy-crochet-buyers-ai-made-140000737.html)
- **Takeaway:** The market has confirmed our core idea (parse a pattern into checkable rows and explain stitches). Every competitor, though, wraps it in an account, cloud AI and a $30-40 a year subscription. There is also community distrust of AI that *invents* patterns, as opposed to AI that *restructures* an existing human-written one.

### Pattern Keeper (Android, cross-stitch analogy)
- **Pricing:** A one-time charge of about $9 after a one-month trial.
- **How patterns get in:** It reads purchased PDF charts, joins multi-page PDFs, highlights by colour, and lets you swipe to mark stitches. It also shows today's and total progress, the percentage done and the "parked thread" corner.
- **Accounts and backup:** No cloud. You move progress through a backup file.
- **Complaints:** Android only (iOS has been "planned" for years), imports can be glitchy, updates are rare, and backstitch isn't tracked. [Knytstudio comparison](https://www.knytstudio.com/blog/cross-stitch-progress-tracker-apps), [Stitchery Corner](https://thestitcherycorner.com/pattern-keeper-cross-stitch-app/)
- **Takeaway:** This is proof that a one-time-price, offline, file-backup tool can become a community favourite if it turns a static pattern into an interactive one and shows satisfying progress.

### StitchFiddle (web, iOS)
- A chart designer for knitting, crochet and cross-stitch. The free tier allows 15 charts, and Premium costs about $2.75 a month. It has light progress tracking on charts and chart-to-written conversion. [Pricing](https://www.stitchfiddle.com/en/premium/pricing/unlimited-charts)
- Relevant mainly for colourwork and C2C. It is not a written-pattern follower.

### The Woobles (kits plus web tutorials)
- Beginner amigurumi kits with about 52 micro-videos per project, short enough that you can "pause mid-stitch without losing your place". Videos come in left- and right-handed versions. Access is on the web with a kit password. [thewoobles.com](https://thewoobles.com/pages/learn-to-crochet-210401), [Reviewed](https://www.reviewed.com/lifestyle/features/woobles-review-easy-way-learn-how-crochet)
- **Takeaway:** Beginners respond to *tiny steps* and stitch help that is always one tap away. Left-handed support is valued.

| Offline use | knitCompanion, Pattern Keeper, BeeCount | Expected; a complaint when missing |

| Screen stays awake while crafting | Most counters | Expected |

## 4. Gaps and pain points

1. **PDFs are poor on phones.** You pinch-zoom, scroll back and forth, and lose your place. "A PDF doesn't remember where you stopped." PDF-markup apps reduce the problem but don't restructure the text. [CrochetPop](https://learn.crochetpop.app/blog/live-row-tracker-track-any-crochet-pattern)
2. **Losing your place and the count**, especially in amigurumi rounds with increase and decrease sequences and nested repeats (`[sc, inc] x 6`, "rep from * around").
3. **Forgetting stitch meanings and abbreviations.** Beginners keep a second tab open. Paid AI explainers exist but meter their use.
4. **US/UK terminology confusion.** The same name means a different stitch (UK dc = US sc, UK tr = US dc), so a project comes out "twice as tall". Few apps detect or convert terms. [KnitPro](https://www.knitpro.eu/en/blog/uk-vs-us-crochet-terminology), [Secret Yarnery](https://secretyarnery.com/blogs/blog/us-vs-uk-crochet-terms-explained-simple-guide-for-all-crocheters)
5. **Multi-size patterns.** Readers have to track one number in every parenthesised list. Only Ribblr (for its own patterns) and a few new AI apps filter by size.
6. **Subscriptions, ads and paywalls.** Ribblr+ costs up to $9.99 a month, Hooked Pro $39.99 a year and My Row Counter $9.99-14.99 a year. Users mention NSFW ads and basic features locked behind paywalls.
7. **Accounts and lock-in.** Ribblr patterns can't be printed or used offline. Cloud-only storage has lost users' PDFs. Privacy is rarely a selling point.
8. **Data loss** after updates or logouts, with no export.
9. **Complexity.** knitCompanion's power comes with an hour-long onboarding.
10. **AI distrust.** The community rejects AI-invented patterns, while AI-*explained* patterns are welcomed.
11. **Accessibility.** The Ravelry incident shows that visual design can physically harm some users.

## 5. Opportunities and differentiators for our app

- **"Bring your own AI", no AI bill.** Users paste the pattern into any chatbot with our prompt and paste the structured result back. This removes the per-use AI cost that drives competitors' $40-a-year subscriptions, so the app can be **free with no ads, ever**.
- **Private by design.** No account and no server: data lives on the device, with explicit export and import of a backup file. Market this against the data-loss and lock-in stories. Prompt users to back up regularly, since that is their only safety net.
- **Faithful structure, not invention.** Position the AI step as *restructuring a human designer's pattern*. Keep the original text visible beside each parsed row so users can check it, and credit and link the source.
- **Size selection at import.** Store per-size values and show only the chosen size.
- **US/UK awareness.** Record the terminology in the format, warn when signals conflict (for example "sc" together with "yoh"), and show both names in the stitch glossary.
- **Stitch help one tap away.** Tapping an abbreviation shows a definition, the US/UK equivalent, the chart symbol and an optional link to an external video. Pattern-specific special stitches come from the pattern's own glossary.
- **First-class repeats.** Nested repeats, "rep rows 3-6 four times" and per-round stitch-count checks ("should have 36 sts").
- **Crafting-friendly UI.** Big tap targets, the screen kept awake, one-handed use, dark mode, a large-text mode, calm visuals (the Ravelry lesson), and no onboarding wall.
- **PWA.** Works on iOS, Android and desktop from a single link, with no app store and no platform gap of the kind Pattern Keeper has.

## 6. Prioritised feature list

### MVP
1. Projects list with several projects in progress; each project has a status and a "resume" button that jumps to the current row.
2. Pattern import by pasting structured text (JSON/YAML-like) produced by a chatbot. Include a copyable "conversion prompt" and validation with friendly errors.
3. Row/round view: tick a row as done, undo, mark the current row, and show the expected stitch count for each row.
4. Repeat tracking (rows repeated N times, in-row repeats shown as an x/N counter).
5. Free-floating row and stitch counters per project.
6. Notes per project and per row.
7. Stitch glossary with tap-to-explain for abbreviations: built-in common stitches plus the pattern's own definitions, marked US or UK.
8. Size selection for multi-size patterns.
9. Local storage (IndexedDB), offline PWA, export and import of a backup file, and reminders to back up.
10. Mobile-first, large-touch, wake-lock, dark mode, and an accessible, calm design.

### Later
- Row reminders ("change colour at row 12") and alerts at row N.
- Linked counters (a row count drives a repeat count), as in BeeCount.
- Original pattern text and photos attached to the project, shown side by side.
- US/UK conversion toggle with a mismatch detector.
- Sections and parts (amigurumi head, body, arms) with per-part progress and an "make 2" multiplier.
- Timer, time per project, and progress percentage and statistics.
- Voice commands or hardware-key (volume button / Bluetooth clicker) counting.
- Yarn and hook details, photos of finished work, and an archive of completed projects.
- Optional sync through a user-owned file (for example a file the user saves to their own cloud folder), with still no server of our own.
- Chart and diagram rendering for simple rounds, and left-handed notes and video links.
